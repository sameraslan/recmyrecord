Can the sonic stop leave the seed's genre?

No option escapes the trade-off between reach and agreement, but CLAP comes out best: a little further across genres, almost the same agreement on how the music feels, and Live-Evil first for Bitches Brew.

Seventeen representations were compared on cached clips. The lists below are the real test: the numbers are all built from RYM genres and descriptors, and RYM descriptors follow genre themselves. Nothing the site serves was changed.

How the measurement works

For every album, take its ten nearest by the audio block alone, with the artist's other records removed. Three numbers per option:

  • Outside the family. How many of the ten are outside the seed's broad genre family (jazz, rock, metal, electronic and so on; 17 families).
  • Descriptor agreement. Mean similarity between the seed's RYM descriptors and its neighbours'. A random list scores 0.23.
  • Crossing quality. Rank only albums outside the seed's family, take the ten nearest, and measure how much better their descriptors match than a random outside-family album. This takes genre out of the comparison.

Random and shuffled blocks sit exactly at the floors, and plain EffNet reproduces the earlier report's figures, so the measurement is doing what it says. Hubs is the skew of how often albums get recommended: under 1 is healthy, above 2 means a few albums are in far too many lists.

What the numbers show

  • EffNet already crosses more than Bitches Brew suggests. On average 3.7 of its ten are outside the seed's family, and those are far better matched than chance. Jazz is the hard case: it forms a tight cluster, and the first non-jazz album for Bitches Brew is at rank 164.
  • No model picks better crossings than EffNet. Forced to choose outside the family, EffNet and CLAP tie, and MusiCNN and MERT are behind. The genre training does not make its out-of-genre picks worse.
  • CLAP reaches a little further for almost nothing. On the whole catalog 4.1 of ten are outside the family against 3.7, for 0.005 less descriptor agreement, and its out-of-family picks agree slightly better than EffNet's. It was trained on audio paired with text, not on genre labels. The price: somewhat more hubs, and at the balanced stop its lists match the seed's primary genre less often (0.237 against 0.270).
  • MERT reaches furthest and agrees least. Its middle layers are its best; the early layers are not better. A few albums take over its lists.
  • Removing the Discogs classifier's directions does nothing. The lists are the same at every rank. Genre is spread through the whole embedding.
  • Removal fitted on RYM genres does move the lists, at a price. Reach rises, the crossings get about as good as adding noise would make them, and hubs appear.
  • The old Spotify lists crossed by being loose. They reached furthest of anything real and had the lowest descriptor agreement. The same scores predicted from audio behave the same way, a little tighter.

Taken together: measured against EffNet diluted with noise to the same reach, nothing is more than about 0.04 better. The options differ mainly in where they sit on one trade-off.

Bitches Brew

Every embedding keeps the list inside jazz. EffNet's first non-jazz album is at rank 164, and Live-Evil, Get Up With It and Rocksession are at 70, 286 and 430.

CLAP also stays inside jazz, but it finds the electric Miles records: Live-Evil is first, At Fillmore and Big Fun are in the ten, and Get Up With It is at 13. Rocksession is at 93 and there is no prog. At the balanced stop the CLAP and EffNet lists share eight of ten.

The old Spotify list had eight of ten outside jazz, with King Crimson twice, at a descriptor match of 0.34 against EffNet's 0.50. The Spotify-like scores predicted from audio give the list closest to what you described: Mahavishnu Orchestra, In a Silent Way, Soft Machine's Third and Julia Holter among free and fusion jazz, at a descriptor match of 0.50. Mixing even a tenth of EffNet back in returns it to all jazz.

One caution on that list: for albums already in the catalog the predictor has seen their real Spotify scores. A new album gets a pure prediction, which recovers energy, acousticness, danceability, loudness and valence well (R² 0.7 to 0.8) and key, mode and tempo poorly.

What to do next

What I would do

Go with CLAP. It has now been run over the whole catalog at four clips per album and is the second column above. It is not trained on genre labels, it keeps almost all of EffNet's agreement, and it finds the Bitches Brew neighbours EffNet missed. Listen first; the numbers are proxies.

If the Spotify-like column sounds right to you, that block can be built today from the clips already stored, with no new downloads. It is the old sonic stop without Spotify: looser than EffNet, further across genres, weaker on the descriptor numbers.

What I would not do

  • Genre erasure fitted on RYM families: weaker crossings and hubs.
  • MERT: least agreement, heaviest to run.
  • Expect CLAP to add prog to Bitches Brew: it does not, and nothing is forced.
  • Pick on these numbers alone. They are RYM proxies; your ears decide.
