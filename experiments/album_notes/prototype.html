What to look at

  1. The year now sits beside the artist. The site has it for every album and shows it nowhere today.
  2. The line (dashed outline) says what makes the record itself, in under 130 characters. It never says the album is acclaimed: every album here is.
  3. Where the words come from is always in view, with AI help said up front, not hidden in a footer.
  4. Read more opens the notes in place: credits, where it comes from, how it was made, what to listen for. The closest albums follow straight after.
  5. Listen for is the part made for the moment the record is playing in another app. Try Películas: a motorcycle is one of the instruments.
  6. Nothing written? Ninjin shows the quiet line used when no source exists. No invented text.

Closed, the block costs about 90 px on a laptop: all five closest albums still fit at 1440 × 900. The recommendations themselves are unchanged and the notes never claim to explain them.

How much of the catalog has something to say

Measured on all 10,467 albums by matching each one to Wikidata (by RYM id, Spotify, Apple Music and Deezer ids, then exact title and artist, then a Wikipedia search checked against the album's artist and title). The plan

  1. Phase 0A trial on 30 albums (rich, translated, thin, across rank bands): notes written in both forms, the plain Wikipedia excerpt and the written notes, then rated by you and two listeners for accuracy and interest. An experiment folder; it changes nothing on the site.
  2. Phase 1The build, after your sign-off: a pipeline step that fetches sources, writes and checks the notes, and saves them with their source revision and licence; the year, the line and Read more on the album page; the empty line; credits on the About page.
  3. Phase 2Richer notes: links to albums in the catalog that the story mentions, a "Same people, other records" strip from credits, the line on the map card and in link previews.
