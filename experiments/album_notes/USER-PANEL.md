recmyrecord, album page (`/album/[slug]`). Run 8 October 2026.

**This is a simulated panel.** All six participants are personas written by Claude, not real people. Use it to sharpen hypotheses and the test plan, not as evidence. Every claim marked "validate" below needs real users.

Where the text would go: the album page today shows cover, artist, title, "Open in Spotify" (plus copy link), the mood descriptor tags, then the list of closest albums with the Sonic / Balanced / Mood slider. On a phone it is one full-screen scrolling panel over the map. The new text would sit between the tags and the list.

## 4. Synthesis

### Verdict

No single concept wins. The winning shape is **D + B's hook, collapsed by default; "Show more" opens C's "Listen for" first and a short, sourced B background second.** A is the fallback source, not a format.

- **D (facts row)** was the only concept nobody objected to. Priya and Tomasz preferred it; Graham, Camila and Daniel called it necessary but not enough. It costs one line, so it never pushes the list down.
- **B's hook works only when it is made of facts.** "Played in a darkened studio for most of a year" got reactions from Leah, Tomasz and Priya. "Mixing ... Beatles pop" annoyed Camila. The difference is specificity, not length.
- **C's "Listen for" was the most valued expansion** (Leah, Graham, Camila, Daniel), because it gives something the page and Wikipedia don't. For Daniel it partly replaces the cover. It is also the riskiest content: one wrong pointer loses Tomasz and Graham for good.
- **B's four sections are too many.** "Why it lasts" was rejected by Tomasz, Graham and Camila (unsourced opinion, cliché magnet). "Making it" was the section people wanted.
- **A, the verbatim excerpt, lost.** It's the dullest part of Wikipedia, sits one tap away anyway, is thinnest on non-English albums (Camila), and a clipped paragraph is just noise for VoiceOver (Daniel).

### Must-haves

1. **Collapsed by default, one line plus the facts row,** above the list but never pushing it more than about 2 lines. No layout shift: reserve the space or render it at build time (the site is static, so build-time text is natural).
2. **Every hook and pointer must be checkable.** Test for each sentence: could it describe another album? If yes, cut it. Ban adjectives like masterpiece, haunting, lush, seminal, tapestry, and "Brazilian rhythms". This fits copy.ts's no-hype rule.
3. **Name track titles, not just numbers,** and match them to the Spotify edition.
4. **Sources per section, linked, with source language shown.** Link to Wikipedia in the album's own language as well as English.
5. **Put the AI label at the top of the expanded text, not the footer.** Add a "Spotted a mistake? Tell us" path.
6. **Accessibility:** real headings, a labelled `<dl>` for the facts (no dot separators), dates written out, `lang` on non-English titles, the button named "More about [album]" with `aria-expanded`, and a way to skip to the list.
7. **Small extras several people asked for:** the sung language plus translated song titles; one "start here" track; vocals or instrumental.

### Anti-patterns

- Generic AI prose. One "lush tapestry" line discredits the recommendations too (Tomasz).
- Verdict sections ("Why it lasts", "influential").
- Foreign-album clichés and exoticism (Camila).
- Fades that clip mid-word.
- Open-by-default sections, or remembering that someone opened it once.
- A label that appears only after the text.
- Padding albums with thin sources. Show only D instead.

### Open questions to validate with real users

1. Do people open "Show more" at all? Log taps and compare: does a hook change how often people press Open in Spotify or click a recommendation?
2. When does reading happen, given that listening happens in another app? Watch phone users switch apps and come back.
3. Does "Checked by a person" earn more trust than "From Wikipedia"? That label carries a cost on 10,000 albums.
4. How many of the 10,000+ albums have enough sourced material for C, by region and language?
5. Is "Listen for" accurate enough without a human listening? Spot-check 50 albums.
6. Licensing: does AI text built mainly on one Wikipedia article carry CC BY-SA share-alike obligations?

### Riskiest assumption

**That generated text can stay specific and correct across the long tail.** The value is highest where English sources are thinnest: non-English albums and lesser-known records. That is exactly where the AI falls back on clichés or invents details. And the panel says one bad sentence costs trust in the whole site, recommendations included. Test this first, before building any UI: generate hooks and pointers for 30 deliberately obscure albums and have knowledgeable listeners grade them.
