# recmyrecord data pipeline

Builds every file the site serves from `frontcreck/public/data/`. The outputs are committed, so the site builds without this pipeline or its inputs.

Since 6 October 2026 the site's data is the 10k catalog: 10,467 albums, the 4,081 of the feature table first and 6,386 new ones after them, built from the EffNet store `audio/effnet10k/` (the owner's go-ahead of that day; `docs/10k-switch.md` is the record of the switch). Until then it was the feature table's 4,081 albums alone, built from `audio/`. That build is still there as `--no-catalog` (see Commands), and the text below says so wherever it describes it.

## Inputs (read-only)

- `data-pipeline/catalog/albums.csv`: the catalog table, one row per album, keyed by RYM id (see Catalog and keys). The existing albums come first, in the feature table's order; a new album's title, artist, descriptors and links come from here. Override with `--catalog-path`.
- `data-retrieval/Recommender/data/all_data_norm.pkl`: the recommender's feature table (4,000+ rows in catalog-rank order; `Title`, `Artist`, `URI`, `Descriptor Count`, 13 min-max Spotify audio features, 176 descriptor columns). It gives the existing albums their title, artist and descriptors. Override with `--table`. The build no longer reads the 13 Spotify columns; only the live-site replica does (see step 2).
- `data-pipeline/audio/effnet10k/`: the committed audio store the site is built from and its frozen transform (see Audio). Override with `--audio-dir`. `data-pipeline/audio/` around it is the first store, which a `--no-catalog` build reads when it is named, and holds `keys.csv`, which gives each Spotify URI of the feature table its album key (see Catalog and keys).
- `data-pipeline/catalog/covers.csv`, `covers_caa.csv`, `covers_skip.csv` and the sprites of `.cache/covers/96/` (not committed, made again from the two tables): the covers of the new albums and of the existing albums whose link changed, and the Cover Art Archive covers of the albums that would have none (see Covers of the new albums).
- `data-pipeline/catalog/unverified_links.csv`: the existing albums that get no Spotify link (see Catalog mode, `s`).
- The music map worktree of the personal site, passed as `--map-root` (for example `/path/to/music_map`). It has the existing albums only. The pipeline reads:
  - `public/data/metadata.json`: cluster id (0 to 7), atlas sheet and UV for each Spotify URI.
  - `public/data/atlas-0.webp` to `atlas-3.webp`: 96 px cover sprites in the map's own order.
  - `pipeline/outputs/spotify_features.parquet`: `cover_url` per `spotify_uri` (the cover source).
- `data-pipeline/overrides.json`: manual corrections (see below).

## Outputs (`frontcreck/public/data/`)

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `albums.json` | Array of 10,467; index is the album id. `{ slug, t, a, s, c, k, d, w }`, then `l` and `n` when the album has them: slug, title, artist, Spotify album id (may be empty), cover id (a Spotify image id, a prefixed id of another source, or empty), cluster 0..7, up to 8 mood descriptor indexes in the order of the album's RYM page, ambient colours `[wash, wash, accent]`, listen links beyond Spotify, `1` for an album without audio |
| `vocab.json` | Mood descriptor words (113) |
| `positions.json` | `{ sonic, balanced, mood }`, flat `[x, y, ...]` arrays in album order, 3 decimals, within [-1, 1] |
| `recs.json` | `{ sonic, balanced, mood }`, 10 album ids per album, closest first; `[]` at sonic and balanced for an album without audio |
| `atlas-0.webp` .. `atlas-10.webp` | 96 px sprites, 1,024 per sheet, album index order (sheet = floor(i / 1024), cell = i % 1024, 32 columns) |
| `thumbs.webp`, `thumbs-1.webp`, `thumbs-2.webp` | 48 px sprites, 64 columns, 4,096 per sheet, album index order |

The fields are described under Catalog mode. On 6 October 2026 the folder is 33 MB (it was 13 MB): `albums.json` 2.45 MB, `recs.json` 1.58 MB, `positions.json` 0.41 MB, eleven atlas sheets 22.9 MB, three thumbnail sheets 5.8 MB. The validator's line for it: `{"albums": 10467, "vocab": 113, "no_cover": 40, "empty_descriptors": 96, "links": 719, "no_audio": 225}` (`no_cover` was 163 until the last-resort covers of 6 October 2026, which gave 123 albums a `c`, 120 of them a sprite and its colours `w` too, and changed nothing else).

A `--no-catalog` build writes the data of before the switch into the folder it is given: 4,081 albums with `{ slug, t, a, s, c, k, d, w }` alone (`c` a Spotify image id or empty, `d` up to 10 indexes), 114 words, 10 ids in every list, `atlas-0.webp` to `atlas-3.webp` and one `thumbs.webp`.

## How it works

1. Albums: every row of `catalog/albums.csv`. The first 4,081 are the feature table's albums (the first row of each Spotify URI, each of them on the map), in its order, so an album's number and slug are what they were; the 6,386 new ones follow. The build stops when the catalog does not start with the table's albums, or when one of them is not on the map. Every album is described by its first 8 descriptors (see Catalog mode, `d`).
2. Recommendations: the live recommender with its 13 Spotify audio columns replaced by the 64-column audio block (see Audio). The matrix is `[audio block | descriptors / slider ** 3]` (184 columns): the 56 lyric and theme descriptors are dropped, the other 120 descriptor columns are divided by `slider ** 3`, and the neighbours are euclidean, at three stops: sonic 5, balanced 1.765 (the original site's tuned default, a divisor of about 5.5), mood 0.5. Ten per album per stop, over the whole catalog; an album without audio has a list at the mood stop only (see Catalog mode). `table.py` keeps an exact replica of the old live recommender (`rec_matrix`, `live_recommend`) and a test checks it: "In Rainbows" at slider 0.5, over the same leading rows the live recommender searches (`LIVE_POOL`), returns Tindersticks, Avalon, So, You Will Never Know Why, Imperial Bedroom. A `--no-catalog` build's mood stop still returns those five (the audio block has almost no weight there). The site's no longer does: with 6,386 more candidates, 8 descriptors and the slope weights, In Rainbows' first five at mood are Glitter, Have You in My Wilderness, Carrie & Lowell Live, Bon Iver, Bon Iver and Takk... (pinned in `tests/test_outputs.py`).
3. Layouts: UMAP (n_neighbors 15, random_state 42; min_dist 0.25 at sonic and balanced, 0.1 at mood) of the same matrix at each stop (at sonic and balanced, of the albums with audio; see Catalog mode, `positions.json`). Groups detached from the main cloud are enlarged to the map's density (UMAP packs them much tighter) and moved next to it, outliers softly compressed, sonic and mood Procrustes-aligned to balanced and resized so the three stops have the same median distance between neighbouring albums, all three scaled into [-1, 1] by one factor, stacked points spread. The site draws covers at one size for every stop, so the stops share a density (a median nearest-neighbour gap of about 0.0049 in the site's map units), not a bounding box. min_dist trades evenness for locality (the numbers that follow were measured on the 4,081-album data, not again on the 10k catalog): with the audio block, 0.1 packs sonic and balanced neighbours into clumps where covers pile up; at 0.25, 59% (sonic) and 44% (balanced) of an album's recommendations are among its 30 nearest albums on the map, against 61% and 48% at 0.1. Mood is already even at 0.1 (42%) and loses the most from a larger value (36% at 0.3).
4. Artists: a new album's artist and title are the catalog's, shown as `native [Latin]` where they are not in Latin script (see Catalog mode, `a` and `t`). For the existing albums: the feature table sometimes glues the member names onto the billed credit with no separator ("Bob Marley & The WailersBob MarleyThe Wailers"). `artists.py` keeps the billed credit: it cuts at the first case or script boundary inside a word when the text before it joins several names ("&", ",", "/", "and", "with", ...) and the glued-on tail repeats one of them. Whitespace is collapsed. Every string it changes in the feature table is listed in `tests/test_artists.py`.
5. Slugs: `kebab(title)-kebab(artist)` from the cleaned artist, ASCII-folded (Cyrillic transliterated), `-2`, `-3` on collision in catalog order. A new album's is made from its romanised title and artist and cut to 120 bytes (see Catalog mode). The build compares its slugs with the committed `albums.json` and stops if one would change: since the switch that covers the new albums too.
6. Vocabulary: kept descriptors minus vocals descriptors, "instrumental" and "concept album", most frequent first, counted on the albums' first 8 descriptors (113 words; "oratorio" is no longer among any album's first 8).
7. Sprites: an existing album's map sprite, or the album's own 96 px sprite (a new album, an existing one whose link changed or with an override image), or a lettered tile when it has no cover, packed in album order; ambient colours from the sprite plus an accent with at least 4.5:1 contrast on `#15110d`. The two washes are the leading colour and the next one of a clearly different hue (or, on a cover with one hue or none, a clearly lighter or darker one), made dark and muted with saturation taken from the colour's chroma so grey covers stay grey; the more visible of the two comes first. The constants in `colors.py` were tuned against the hand-picked pairs of the design mockup.

## Audio

The audio block is 64 numbers per album computed from 30-second preview clips (the evidence is in `experiments/preview_features/REPORT.md`, the design in `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-10-02-preview-audio-design.md`). It has two stages. The audio stage (`rmr_audio`, see The audio stage below) matches an album on Deezer or iTunes, runs each clip through Essentia's Discogs-EffNet model and stores the album's mean embedding; it is rare, needs the network and its own environment. The build stage (`rmr_pipeline`) only reads the store, so anyone can rebuild the site data from committed files.

The site is built from `audio/effnet10k/` (see The 10k EffNet store). `audio/` itself, described first, is the store the site was built from until 6 October 2026: the feature table's albums at up to eight clips. Nothing on the site reads its embeddings any more; it stays in place, with `audio/clap/`, until the owner decides what happens to them (`docs/10k-switch.md`, section 8). The files `keys.csv`, `matches.csv` and `match_overrides.json` in it are shared by all three stores.

### The first store (`data-pipeline/audio/`, committed)

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `embeddings/part-NNNN.npz` | Append-only shards: `keys` (the album's key: its RYM id, see Catalog and keys), `emb` (float16, 1,280 per album: the mean of its clips' embeddings), `n_clips`, `source` (`deezer`, `itunes:us`, `local`, ...). A key in a later shard replaces the earlier one; an entry with `n_clips` 0 removes the album. |
| `manifest.json` | Model id, clip policy (`clips.per_album`: how many clips a plain `sync` gives an album), one entry per shard. |
| `matches.csv` | One row per album: source, source album id, matched title and artist, score, `ambiguous`, track count, clips available; then `matched_by` (`deezer_id` or `apple_id`: the catalog row's link; `search`; `override`), `edition` (`standard` or `split` when the edition rule replaced the linked listing), `runtime_s` and the flags `under_covered` and `short_preview` (1, 0, or empty: not determined). An empty source means no match. The five later columns are empty for the albums matched before 3 October 2026. |
| `match_overrides.json` | Hand corrections for the audio stage: key -> `{"source", "album_id"}` to force a match, or `{"skip": true}`. |
| `keys.csv` | One row per album that had a Spotify URI as its key: `rym_id` (the key), `legacy_uri`, `matched_by`, `doubt`. Written by the catalog builder. |
| `transform.npz` | The frozen transform: `mean`, `components` (64 x 1,280), `scale`, `target_total_variance`, and the fit's date, album count and model id. |

`audio_store.py` reads and writes these and rejects a malformed store with a message naming the file. It needs only numpy (1.26 or 2.x), so the audio stage can use it from its own environment.

The store began as 3,944 albums with four clips each, migrated from the experiment's cache by `scripts/migrate_experiment_audio.py`. Against the experiment's own block the migrated one differed by at most 0.0002 (values reach 0.6), because the store keeps float16 means. On 2 October 2026 `rmr_audio sync --clips 8 --retry-unmatched` topped every album up to eight clips where its listing has that many (13,103 clips, 105 minutes of work with two workers) and added 36 albums; the shards were compacted into one and the transform refitted. The store now holds 3,980 albums: 3,182 with eight clips, 798 with fewer because their listing has fewer previews.

Every file of the store is written under a temporary name and renamed into place, and a shard is in place before the manifest lists it. A crash leaves at worst a temporary file or an unlisted shard; the store still loads, `rmr_audio status` names the leftovers, and the next `sync` or `compact` removes them.

### The 10k EffNet store (`data-pipeline/audio/effnet10k/`)

The owner listened on 5 October 2026 and chose EffNet over CLAP for the 10k catalog, and on 6 October 2026 the site's data was built from it: `SITE_MODEL = "effnet10k"` in `rmr_pipeline/audio_store.py`. `audio/effnet10k/` is the Discogs-EffNet store for the whole catalog: 10,242 of the 10,467 albums (Deezer 6,475, Apple 2,893, YouTube 874), four clips per album (99 albums on previews have fewer, 27 of them one; an album on windows of full-length audio has 4 to 8), with its own `embeddings/`, `manifest.json` and `transform.npz`. It shares `keys.csv`, `matches.csv` and `match_overrides.json` with `audio/`. Like the CLAP store below it is written whole from the one-pass clip cache, by the same command and the same pooling rule, and can be written again at any time:

```bash
cd data-pipeline
nice -n 19 .venv/bin/python -m rmr_audio.modelstore write --model effnet --audio-dir audio/effnet10k --clips 4 --exclude Album999417,Album739618
nice -n 19 .venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.audio fit-catalog --audio-dir audio/effnet10k   # audio/effnet10k/transform.npz
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_audio.modelstore status --model effnet --audio-dir audio/effnet10k --exclude Album999417,Album739618
```

`--model effnet` needs `--audio-dir`. `write` refuses `audio/` itself, whatever the model: that store is `rmr_audio sync`'s. `--exclude` is as for the CLAP store, and has to be passed on every write.

`audio/` is untouched by it: 3,980 albums at up to eight clips, with a transform fitted on the feature table's albums. For the albums still on preview clips the cache's eight-clip means are the vectors of `audio/` bit for bit, except the seven whose listing was corrected by hand. So the new store differs from `audio/` by the clip count (four, not eight), by the seven corrected listings and by the 100 albums that are now on YouTube windows. The cosine between the two stores' vectors has a median of 0.979 over the 3,978 albums in both.

What the switch of 6 October 2026 did:

- the build reads the store by default (`SITE_MODEL`), and is the catalog build by default. 4,042 of the 4,081 existing albums have audio there, and 6,200 of the 6,386 new ones;
- the store's reference was recorded in `tests/fixtures/catalog_audio_reference.npz` (`scripts/record_audio_reference.py --catalog --out ...`): every catalog album's store row and audio block. `tests/test_record_reference.py` compares the store with it, bit for bit. Record it again only after a change meant to move them (a store written from other clips, a refit). `tests/fixtures/audio_reference.npz` is still the reference of `audio/` and `tests/test_rekey.py` still reads it;
- the site data was rebuilt (see Outputs). A test fails when `recs.json` is not what the committed store, the catalog and the build's default flags give.

### The CLAP store (`data-pipeline/audio/clap/`) and the switch

A store holds one model's embeddings; its manifest names the model and the width (`dim`), and `audio_store.py` checks every shard and the transform against it. `audio/` is the Discogs-EffNet store (1,280 numbers per album). `audio/clap/` is the store of `laion/larger_clap_music_and_speech` (512), with its own `embeddings/`, `manifest.json` and `transform.npz`. It is written from the clip cache's `clap_mp3` rows (the variant below), so its manifest names the model as `laion/larger_clap_music_and_speech+mp3-128k-stereo`. It shares `keys.csv`, `matches.csv` and `match_overrides.json` with the store it sits in.

The site build reads the store named by `SITE_MODEL` in `rmr_pipeline/audio_store.py`. It is `"effnet10k"` (`"effnet"`, the store `audio/`, until 6 October 2026). Setting it to `"clap"` would make `rmr_pipeline.build` and `rmr_pipeline.audio status` read `audio/clap/` when no `--audio-dir` is given; nothing changes on the site until the data is rebuilt and committed, and `tests/fixtures/catalog_audio_reference.npz` then has to be recorded again.

The CLAP store is not appended to. It is written whole from the one-pass clip cache, for every album of `catalog/albums.csv` that has an ok `clap_mp3` clip, and can be written again at any time:

```bash
cd data-pipeline
nice -n 19 .venv/bin/python -m rmr_audio.modelstore write --model clap_mp3 --audio-dir audio/clap --exclude Album999417,Album739618   # audio/clap/embeddings, manifest.json
nice -n 19 .venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.audio fit-catalog --audio-dir audio/clap   # audio/clap/transform.npz
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_audio.modelstore status --exclude Album999417,Album739618
```

The CLAP store stays on the branch. It is not the store the site uses: see the section above.

`--exclude Album999417,Album739618` leaves out A Clockwork Orange and Barry Lyndon: the only audio the cache has for them is of other records (a wrong YouTube pick and an unrelated store listing), and no right source was found. The flag is not remembered anywhere, so pass it on every `write`, or the two albums come back into the store.

Both need only numpy, load no model and download nothing. Without `--model` and `--audio-dir`, `write` would put the cache's plain `clap` rows in `audio/clap/`; `tests/test_modelstore.py` fails on such a store. `write` opens the cache read-only and writes nothing when the store already holds exactly what the cache and the catalog give. An album vector is the mean of the album's first four ok clips in rank order (a failed clip does not use up a place; an album with fewer uses what it has; windows of full-length audio replace the previews and all of them count), from one listing, kept as float16 with `n_clips` and `source`. The listing is the one `match_overrides.json` forces when it has an ok clip for the model, else the one `matches.csv` names, else the one with the most ok clips. `matches.csv` records what the EffNet store was embedded from, so for an album whose listing was corrected by hand it still names the old listing; the override comes first. While the override's listing is not embedded, the album stays on the other listing and `write` (and `--dry-run`) prints how many such albums there are, with their keys. `write --exclude KEY[,KEY...]` leaves albums out of the store (an album whose only audio is of another record) and names them in its first line; `status --exclude` with the same keys says whether they are in the store. For the Deezer albums the earlier CLAP run covered it is `clap_catalog.load(4)`'s vector to 1e-8 (a Deezer clip's `clap_mp3` vector is its `clap` vector). A cache key the catalog no longer has is followed through `keys.csv`.

`fit-catalog` fits the 64 components on every catalog album the store has, the new ones included, and never writes `audio/transform.npz`. It keeps the EffNet transform's `target_total_variance` (0.3905): `scale` brings any model's block to that total, and the slider stops were tuned against a block of that size.

#### The `clap_mp3` variant (beside `clap` in the clip cache; what `audio/clap/` is written from)

CLAP hears the store a preview came from: Deezer's previews are 128 kbit/s stereo MP3, Apple's are AAC (`experiments/audio_10k/results/store_effect_fix.md`). `clap_mp3` is a second CLAP vector per clip, kept in the cache's `embeddings` table under its own model name and never in place of `clap`. For a clip that is not from Deezer (`itunes:*`, `youtube`, `bandcamp`, `local`) the decoded audio, with its channels, goes through a 128 kbit/s stereo MP3 round trip (`rmr_audio/mp3trip.py`: ffmpeg `libmp3lame` through pipes, no file) before the channels are averaged and the CLAP recipe runs unchanged; a one-channel signal is duplicated to both sides for the encode. For a Deezer clip the variant is the `clap` vector itself, copied inside the cache. `audio/clap/` is written from it; `results/mp3_variant_check.md` in the same folder has what was measured.

```bash
cd data-pipeline
.venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio.onepass copy                                   # Deezer clips: the clap rows, copied; no network, no model
nice -n 19 .venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio.onepass run --models clap_mp3 --itunes-interval 3.4   # the other preview clips: fetched again, clap_mp3 only
nice -n 19 .venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio.fulllength --models effnet,clap,clap_mp3   # full-length windows: all three from one download; embedded albums are fetched once more for clap_mp3 alone
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_audio.modelstore write --model clap_mp3 --audio-dir audio/clap --exclude Album999417,Album739618   # the CLAP store, from the variant's rows (any other folder to look at it first)
```

`run --models clap_mp3` takes, per album, the clips that are ok for `clap` (the clips its mean is over), downloads each once and embeds it for the variant only: EffNet and `clap` are not computed, the EffNet child is not started, and no `effnet` or `clap` row is written. A `clap` clip whose preview the store no longer lists is recorded as `no_preview` ("gone") and the next track in the usual order stands in; the run prints both counts. `--check-baseline` also embeds each fetched clip for `clap` and prints its cosine with the stored vector, without storing it. A new album is embedded with `--models effnet,clap,clap_mp3`: one download per clip.

#### Full-length audio: a link given by hand, the sheet's link, a search, the edge cases

`rmr_audio.fulllength` is the one command for audio that does not come from store previews. It fetches a whole album with yt-dlp, embeds 30-second windows of it (8 laid out, 4 to 8 in the mean by runtime) and deletes the file; the outcome of every album is a row of `audio/fulllength.csv`. The rules are in the module's docstring.

```bash
cd data-pipeline
M="--models effnet,clap,clap_mp3"
nice -n 19 .venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio.fulllength $M                          # albums without previews: the sheet's YouTube link
nice -n 19 .venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio.fulllength $M --retry-failed           # only the rows that failed (a download refused)
nice -n 19 .venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio.fulllength $M --search                 # and a YouTube search where the link is missing or not the album
nice -n 19 .venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio.fulllength $M --edge-cases --search    # albums on 1 to 3 previews: full-length windows replace them
nice -n 19 .venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio.fulllength $M --links-only --dry-run   # the links given by hand: what each one is (metadata only)
nice -n 19 .venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio.fulllength $M --links-only             # fetch and embed them
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_audio.album_status                                            # the table, after any of them
```

- **Links given by hand** (`audio/fulllength_links.csv`: `key`, `url`, `note`). A link the owner found for an album: a YouTube video, a YouTube playlist of per-track videos, or a Bandcamp page. One row per album. Every run reads the file and stops before doing anything when it is wrong (a key the catalog does not have, two rows for one album, a link that is neither YouTube's nor Bandcamp's). A hand-given link comes before the sheet's link and the search, and is tried even when the album's other rows say unavailable, mismatch or `search_none`. It is taken for the album without being judged: title, uploader and length are recorded and decide nothing. It is still refused when there is nothing to fetch (removed, private, live) or it is no album by its size (over six hours, over 200 videos); then the album's other links get their turn in the next run. `watch?v=..&list=..` is read as the playlist, and `pp=`, `si=` and the like are dropped. The row has `matched_by = manual`. Change the link in the file and the next run fetches the new one. `--links-only` works on the albums of the file alone; with `--dry-run` it asks yt-dlp for each link's metadata (one request a link, nothing downloaded) and prints what it is.
- **Playlists**. A YouTube playlist is used only when it was given by hand; a sheet link or a search result that is a playlist is still a mismatch. Its videos are downloaded one file each, in playlist order, with a short pause between two, and deleted after embedding like any other file. The 8 windows are shared among the files by duration, so at most 8 of the videos are embedded; a video under 30 seconds that gets a window is that window whole. A video that is gone is passed over. Any other error fails the whole album, and nothing of it is embedded.
- **Search** (`--search`). For an album whose sheet link is missing, unavailable, a single track or another video, yt-dlp's `ytsearch10:` is asked in up to three forms (titles, uploaders and lengths only; no API key, no account, no cookies). A video is taken only when its title has the album's title, its title or uploader has the artist, its length fits (within 15% of the store listing's runtime when the album has one; else 25 minutes or more, 8 when the title says "full album"), its title does not say live, cover, review, remix and the like, and something more speaks for it: "full album" in the title, the artist's own channel, the listing's runtime, or several uploads of the same length. When two videos of different lengths score alike, nothing is taken. The row records `matched_by = search`, the query, the score and the runner-up's; when nothing is taken, `status = search_none` with the video that came closest and why it was refused, and the album is not searched again unless `--retry-search`. Everything is judged from metadata: nobody listens.
- **Edge cases** (`--edge-cases`). The albums that have previews and are under-covered by them: 1 to 3 previews of a listing that runs 15 minutes or more, or whose runtime is unknown (`album_status.edge_case`; Long Season is one track with one preview). The sheet's link is used when it is the album, else the search. The windows replace the previews in the album's mean; the preview clips stay in the cache, and an album nothing is found for keeps them. The row has `reason = edge_case`.
- Not done: when the video a search took is unavailable the next best one is not tried.

#### Where each album stands

`audio/album_status.csv` has one row per catalog album: its listing, its ok clips per model and the source its mean is taken from, its flags, and a `state` (`done`, `partial`, `no_audio`) and a `next_step` (`youtube_search` and `youtube_full_length` until the search or the edge-case run has been through the album; `none_available` when they found nothing; `manual_link` while a link given by hand is still to fetch). The column `manual_link` has the outcome of that link. `audio/album_status.md` has the counts. `.venv/bin/python -m rmr_audio.album_status` writes both from the catalog, `matches.csv`, `match_overrides.json`, `fulllength.csv`, `fulllength_links.csv` and the clip cache (read-only; it fails without it), and gives the same bytes for the same inputs, so run it again after any audio job. The columns and the rules are in the module's docstring.

### The block

`block = ((e / |e|) - mean) @ components.T * scale`, where `e` is the album's stored embedding. The transform is a PCA fitted on the store's albums: the site's, `audio/effnet10k/transform.npz`, on the four-clip means of all 10,242 albums of that store (5 October 2026); `audio/transform.npz` on the eight-clip means of the 3,980 albums of `audio/`. `scale` gives the block the total variance the 13 Spotify columns had on the albums of the first fit (`target_total_variance`, 0.3905), so the slider stops keep their meaning.

A transform is frozen. Refit only on purpose, and name the store:

```bash
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.audio fit-catalog --audio-dir audio/effnet10k   # the site's: every catalog album the store has
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.audio fit --audio-dir audio                      # the first store: the feature table's albums
```

`fit` has no default store since the switch: without `--audio-dir` it stops, because on the site's store it would replace the transform fitted on the whole catalog with one fitted on the feature table's albums alone. A refit moves every block and every map position, so it is never automatic. It keeps `target_total_variance`. Rebuild and commit the site data and record the reference again after it. `tests/test_modelstore.py` wants the site's transform fitted on exactly the store's albums, so an album added to `audio/effnet10k` means a refit; whether that is wanted for each addition is not decided (`docs/10k-switch.md`, section 8).

The paragraph below is about `audio/`.

What the top-up and the refit of 2 October 2026 moved, against the four-clip data: at the sonic stop an album keeps 5.3 of its 10 recommendations on average, at the balanced stop 7.8, at the mood stop 9.9. Nearly all of it is the top-up (5.4, 7.8, 9.9 with the old transform kept); the refit alone moves 0.5, 0.2 and 0.0 of 10. Four clips were a noisy sample of an album: the mean embedding of an album moved to a cosine of 0.978 (median) with its four-clip mean.

### Albums without audio

225 of the site's 10,467 albums have no audio in `audio/effnet10k`: 39 existing and 186 new (223 the one-pass cache has no usable clip for, and A Clockwork Orange and Barry Lyndon, left out with `--exclude`). Nothing is imputed for them. Such an album has `n` in `albums.json`, a list at the mood stop only, and is in no other album's sonic or balanced list (see Catalog mode); the site says that sound matches are not available for it. `audio/album_status.csv` says where each album stands, and the build prints the counts.

Imputation is what the site did until 6 October 2026, and what a `--no-catalog` build still does. On `audio/`, 101 of the 4,081 albums have no audio: 98 have no listing with previews on Deezer or in the US, British or German iTunes store, and 3 are kept out by hand in `match_overrides.json` (the stores only have cover versions or another album). There each still needs a row, so its block is imputed: the mean block of its 3 nearest albums by descriptor distance among the albums with audio, rescaled to those neighbours' mean norm. `python -m rmr_pipeline.audio status --audio-dir audio` lists them; without `--audio-dir` it reads the site's store and lists the 39 existing albums a `--no-catalog` build on that store would impute.

The imputation settings come from `experiments/preview_features/imputation.py` (`results/imputation.md`), which hides the audio of 137 random albums that have it, five times, and compares their imputed lists with their real ones. With k = 3 and rescaling a hidden album gets back 8% of its real top 10 at the sonic stop and 42% at the balanced stop; the share of its recommendations with its primary genre is 0.15 (0.25 with its own audio) at sonic and 0.18 (0.28) at balanced; it appears in 14 lists at sonic and 12 at balanced, where an average album appears in 10. Without rescaling the mean block is shorter than a real one and the album lands in 28 and 19 lists. Larger k does not recover more and makes the imputed albums recommend each other.

### Adding albums

Since the switch a new album is a row of the sheet, not of the feature table. No album has been added this way since the 10k catalog was built, so the steps are the ones the 6,386 went through, not a tested routine:

1. The catalog: export the sheet's chart tab and run `python -m rmr_catalog` (see Catalog and keys). The new album gets a row after the existing ones, with its descriptors (`top_descriptors`) and links. A descriptor name the feature table has no column for is dropped unless `catalog/descriptor_aliases.json` names its column.
2. Audio: `python -m rmr_audio match-new`, the one-pass cache and `rmr_audio.fulllength` for an album without previews, then `modelstore write --model effnet --audio-dir audio/effnet10k ...` and `fit-catalog --audio-dir audio/effnet10k` (see The 10k EffNet store). The refit moves every album's block; see The block. An album left without audio is built mood-only.
3. Covers: `python -m rmr_pipeline.covers refs`, then `sprites` (see Covers of the new albums). `refs` ends with the last resort for the albums the other sources left without a cover.
4. Rebuild (`rmr_pipeline.build --require-sprites`), validate, and record `tests/fixtures/catalog_audio_reference.npz` again if the store or the transform changed.
5. Commit the catalog, the store, `matches.csv`, `match_overrides.json`, `covers.csv`, the reference and the regenerated site data. A test fails when `recs.json` is not what the committed store and catalog give. Nothing under `.cache/` is committed.

The new album needs nothing from the map inputs: its cluster is taken from its nearest existing albums and its sprite from `.cache/covers/96`. The sheets grow by themselves (1,024 albums per atlas sheet, 4,096 per thumbnail sheet); the frontend has the same constants.

For `audio/` and a `--no-catalog` build the old steps still hold: a row in the feature table (descriptors, a unique `URI`, title, artist; the table is a pickle pinned by hash in `DEFAULT_TABLE_SHA256` of `rmr_pipeline/constants.py`) and a key for that URI in `audio/keys.csv`, then `rmr_audio sync`, which matches the album, embeds its clips, writes one new shard of `audio/` and adds the album's row to `matches.csv`; `rmr_audio status --missing` lists the albums without audio. An existing album cannot be added to the site this way any more: the catalog must start with the feature table's albums, so a new table row needs the catalog builder too.

### The audio stage (`rmr_audio`)

It has its own environment, because the arm64 Essentia wheel needs Python 3.11 and `numpy<2` while the build pins numpy 2.5 on Python 3.12:

```bash
uv venv --python 3.11 data-pipeline/.venv-audio      # or python3.11 -m venv
uv pip install --python data-pipeline/.venv-audio/bin/python -r data-pipeline/requirements-audio.txt
cd data-pipeline
.venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio status [--missing]    # counts; with --missing, the albums without audio
.venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio sync --dry-run        # what a sync would do; no network, nothing written
.venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio match [--retry-unmatched]   # ask the stores, print each verdict; the store is not written
.venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio match-dry-run --sample 300 --seed 1 --out DIR   # match a sample of the new albums; writes only DIR
nice -n 19 .venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio sync       # albums missing from the store, at the store's clip policy
nice -n 19 .venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio sync --clips 8    # top every album up to 8 clips
.venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio compact               # rewrite all shards into one
.venv-audio/bin/python -m pytest tests_audio              # its tests (no network)
```

The venv is about 560 MB. The model (`discogs-effnet-bs1-1.pb`, 18 MB) is fetched into `.cache/audio/models/` on the first sync, from the experiment's cache if it is there, else from essentia.upf.edu, and checked against a pinned SHA-256.

`sync` works on every album of the feature table that is missing from the store or has fewer clips than asked. For each it reads the match from `matches.csv` (searching the stores only for an album never matched), fetches the track listing again (Deezer's preview URLs expire after 15 minutes), downloads the clips it still needs into memory, embeds them and records each clip in the local clip cache. At the end it writes one new shard with the mean of each finished album's clips and updates `matches.csv`. An album whose clips did not change is not rewritten.

- Clips: the first track, then tracks spread evenly through the album, so an album's four clips are the first four of its eight. `--clips N` asks for N; without it the store's policy (`clips.per_album` in `manifest.json`) applies. A `--clips` run over the whole catalog that finishes makes N the policy. An album with fewer previews than N gets what there is. A run that left albums unfinished (a store not answering, an interruption, `--limit`, `--keys`) does not change the policy.
- Resuming: Ctrl-C or SIGTERM stops taking albums, lets the clips in flight finish (a few seconds; a wait between API retries ends at once) and writes the shard for the albums that finished. Running `sync` again continues; no clip is downloaded twice. `--limit N` stops after N albums, `--keys` takes album keys or slugs (a Spotify URI of before the rekey is understood).
- The clip cache (`.cache/audio/clips.sqlite`, gitignored) holds every clip's embedding, by album key. A cache written before 3 October 2026 knows the albums by their Spotify URI and was not rekeyed with the store: until it is, a sync finds none of its clips and downloads again what a top-up needs. `rmr_pipeline.keys.load_keys().legacy(key)` gives the URI such a cache knows an album by, `.current(uri)` the key. It is what lets a top-up from four to eight clips download only four. If it is lost nothing committed is lost; a top-up then downloads all eight. `python -m rmr_audio import-experiment` seeds it from the experiment's cache (only on the machine that ran the experiment) and, while the store still has its first shard, checks that those clips reproduce it exactly.
- Load: 2 worker processes by default (`--workers`), one thread each, at the lowest priority. Measured on an M-series laptop: about 2 clips a second, 600 to 770 MB per worker and 1.4 to 1.6 GB in all. The laptop must stay awake: a sleeping machine pauses the run and the preview URLs fetched before the sleep expire (those clips fail and the next sync fetches them); `caffeinate -i` in front of the command prevents idle sleep on macOS. No audio is written to disk beyond a temp file deleted right after decoding.
- Failures: API errors and Deezer's quota answer are retried with backoff (Deezer is asked at most 5 times a second, iTunes once every 3.2 seconds; four attempts per URL). A store that fails three URLs in a row is left alone for the rest of the run, and the albums that needed it are listed and left for the next run. A clip that cannot be downloaded or decoded is recorded with its status. A preview that is empty, too short or undecodable is final, and the next track in the clip order takes its place, so the album still gets its N clips when it has enough previews. A download that failed is tried again by the next sync; a fresh URL refused (HTTP 4xx) in two runs is final. An album with no usable clip stays out of the store and is listed at the end. A worker that crashes costs one clip.

**Matching.** The audio stage works on every row of `catalog/albums.csv`: the site's albums, then the chart's new ones by rank. An album that already has a row in `matches.csv` is not matched again.

*By link.* When the album's catalog row has a Deezer or an Apple Music link (the ones RYM lists), that listing is fetched by its id and accepted, with no search: the RYM community chose it. Deezer's link comes first. An Apple id only resolves in its own storefront, so it is asked for in the storefront the link names (`music.apple.com/jp/...` is `jp`), then in `us`; the source records the storefront that answered (`itunes:jp`). The listing is not verified. Its title and artist are compared with the album's native and Latin spellings, and `ambiguous` is set when either reads below the search's floors; the listing is kept either way. The edition rule then looks at the same artist's other listings of the same release in that store: a linked listing with more than 30 tracks or a bigger-edition marker (deluxe, expanded, box set...) gives way to the standard edition when there is one (15 minutes or longer, with previews), and a linked listing of one to three tracks gives way to a split edition of the same recording, the one with the most previews among those with more tracks whose total runtime is within 15% of the linked one. `edition` says `standard` or `split` when that happened. When the linked listing has no preview, the other store's link is tried, then the text search; if none has a preview the album is recorded with its linked listing and no clips.

*Flags.* `under_covered`: one to three preview windows for a listing of 15 minutes or more. `short_preview`: a preview shorter than 25 s from a track longer than 60 s. Neither store's API gives a preview's length, so the matcher leaves it empty; the embedder, which decodes the clips, sets it (`match.short_preview`). Two albums matched to one store listing are printed after a `sync` (`duplicate listing`; `audio_store.duplicate_listings`).

*Dry run.* `match-dry-run` matches a random sample of the new on-chart albums, stratified by rank, and writes `matches_dry_run.csv`, `summary.md` and `summary.json` into `--out`: counts by path, the no-preview, ambiguous and under-covered rates, what the edition rule did, duplicate listings, API errors, the distribution of preview windows and a projection of requests and time for all new albums. It reads store metadata only (no audio), writes neither the store nor `matches.csv`, asks Deezer every 0.35 s and iTunes every 3.4 s, and stops when a store stops answering. Its rates say what the stores list, not whether a listing is the right recording.

*The new albums.* `match-new` matches every catalog album that has no row in `matches.csv` and writes the rows, in catalog order, after the existing ones, which are written back unchanged. It reads store metadata only: no audio, no model. It works through the albums with a Deezer link, then those with an Apple link only, then those with no link (`--links-only` leaves these out; `--limit N` takes the first N). `matches.csv` is rewritten through a temporary file every 25 albums (`--save-every`), at the end and on Ctrl-C or SIGTERM, and the API responses are cached, so a killed run continues where it stopped and asks for nothing twice. It asks Deezer every 0.35 s and iTunes every 3.4 s (`--deezer-interval`, `--itunes-interval`), tries a URL twice, and stops (exit code 2, the reason in its last lines) when a store is marked down, refuses a URL twice for its rate (HTTP 403 or 429, Deezer's quota error) or has given five such answers in the run. It prints a progress line with an ETA every minute (`--progress-secs`; `--verbose` adds a line per album) and ends with the counts by path, the albums without a preview, the ambiguous and under-covered ones and the duplicate listings. An album with `skip` in `match_overrides.json` gets no row. The dry run projects about 11 hours for all 6,429 new albums, of which the 804 without a link take more than half.

```bash
nice -n 19 .venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_audio match-new --links-only   # then again without --links-only
```

*By text search*, for an album with neither link (or whose linked listings have no preview). The catalog's Latin spellings and each name of an " & " credit are searched for as well, and `jp` is asked after the storefronts given. Deezer first; iTunes when Deezer has no match, a doubtful one, or previews for fewer than half the tracks. The iTunes storefronts are tried in order (`--storefronts`, by default `us,gb,de`): the first one as the fallback, each further one only while there is still no confident match with a preview. A probe of nine storefronts over the albums the US store lacks found 16 of them elsewhere, all of them in the British or the German store; pass others (`jp`, `br`, `pl`...) explicitly when wanted. Album ids are the same in every storefront. When a search finds the artist but not the album, the artist's album list is read as well (search misses albums the store has). The source is recorded as `deezer` or `itunes:<storefront>`. Albums recorded as unmatched are not looked at again unless `--retry-unmatched` is given (or the album is named in `--keys`). API responses are cached for 30 days in `.cache/audio/http.sqlite`; error answers are never cached. With `--retry-unmatched` a cached search older than a day is asked again, so a `match --retry-unmatched` followed the same day by `sync --retry-unmatched` asks only once.

What the matcher will not do by itself, and an override does: an album listed under another credit ("The Grand Wazoo" under Frank Zappa, not The Mothers), under another title ("Pappo's Blues, Vol. 3" for "Vol. 3"), or a classical recording listed with its composer in front ("Beethoven: Symphony No. 5" for "Symphonie Nr.5": the title alone does not say whose fifth it is, so this is checked by hand against the track list and the durations). Game soundtracks on Apple are mostly cover versions; two are kept out with `skip`.

**Match overrides** (`audio/match_overrides.json`, keyed by the album's key):

```json
{
  "Album45": { "source": "deezer", "album_id": "1261474", "note": "the match was a tribute album" },
  "Album974": { "source": "itunes:jp", "album_id": "826492492" },
  "sp:4Hbe1M0BDbgMwbw6Tw2fmD": { "skip": true, "note": "no store has the right recording" }
}
```

Precedence, highest first: `skip`; a folder given with `--local-dir`; a forced listing that is not yet the one in `matches.csv`; local audio already in the store; the listing in `matches.csv`. A forced listing replaces the album's embedding at the next sync (with or without `--retry-unmatched`), also when that embedding came from local files; if the forced listing has no preview the old embedding is removed. `skip` keeps the album out of matching, and an embedding it already has is removed at the next sync (the shard gets an entry with `n_clips` 0, and `compact` then forgets the album). This is how a wrong embedding is taken out: add `{"skip": true, "note": "..."}` and run `sync`. Every entry carries a `note` saying what was checked.

**Local files.** For an album no store has: put its files in `DIR/<album slug>/` (mp3, m4a, flac, wav, ogg, aiff; file-name order is track order) and run `sync --local-dir DIR`. `status --missing` prints the slugs as `slug<TAB>artist — title`. One excerpt per file: 30 seconds starting 30 seconds in, centred when the track is shorter than a minute, the whole file when it is shorter than 30 seconds (under 5 seconds is skipped). A folder takes precedence over the stores for that album, the source is recorded as `local`, and a later sync without `--local-dir` leaves the album alone. Adding, removing or replacing a file updates the album at the next `sync --local-dir`. The files are only read.

## Catalog and keys

An album's key is its RateYourMusic id (`Album45`), in the catalog table, the audio store, `matches.csv`, `match_overrides.json` and `transform.npz`. Until 3 October 2026 it was the feature table's Spotify URI; `audio/keys.csv` says which key each URI became, and the build and the audio stage find an album's store row through it. The Spotify id stays what the site links to.

| File | Contents |
|---|---|
| `catalog/albums.csv` | Every album, keyed by `rym_id`: the site's albums first, in their order (row number = album number on the site), then the chart's new albums by rank. `rank`, `on_chart`, `artist`, `title`, `artist_latin`, `title_latin`, `rym_artist`, `rym_title` (the sheet's spellings), `year`, `release_date`, `type`, genres, `top_descriptors`, the Spotify, Apple Music, Deezer, Bandcamp, YouTube and SoundCloud links RYM lists, `rym_url`, `legacy_uri`. |
| `audio/keys.csv` | `rym_id`, `legacy_uri`, `matched_by` (`spotify_id`, `artist_title_year`, `manual`, `none`), `doubt` (the reasons the album is in the doubtful list). One row per existing album, one key per row. |
| `catalog/doubtful_pairs.csv` | Old-to-new pairs to decide by hand, the undecided first: both sides' artist, title, year and links, the reason, and what the builder did (`default`: `paired` or `unpaired`). |
| `catalog/manifest.json` | The sheet export the catalog was built from (SHA-256, retrieval dates) and the counts. |

The catalog only grows. An existing album that is not on the chart stays, with `on_chart` 0. It has no RYM id until its page is read, so its key is the placeholder `sp:<Spotify album id>`. An existing album keeps the feature table's title and artist, because the site's slugs are made from them. The build takes its albums from the catalog table, and the existing albums' title, artist and descriptors from the feature table (a `--no-catalog` build takes everything from the feature table). The audio stage reads every row of `catalog/albums.csv`, the new ones included (see Matching): a plain `rmr_audio sync` matches and embeds them.

```bash
cd data-pipeline
.venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_catalog            # rewrites catalog/ and audio/keys.csv from the sheet export
.venv-audio/bin/python -m rmr_catalog --check    # writes nothing; fails when the committed files are stale
.venv/bin/python scripts/rekey_audio_store.py    # brings the store's keys to keys.csv; does nothing when they are current
```

The builder reads a CSV export of the sheet's "Top 10K Chart" tab (`experiments/audio_10k/cache/rym10k_sheet.csv` by default, not committed; `--sheet` for another), the feature table, `overrides.json` and the old scrape (for release years). It runs in the audio venv because it folds names with `rmr_audio/textnorm.py`. It uses no network.

**The pairing rule** (`rmr_catalog/pairing.py`). The sheet's own `in_recmyrecord` flag is not used. A chart row is an existing album when:

1. it links the album's Spotify id (the `s` of `overrides.json` where the URI is known to be another album) and the names do not contradict it: artist and title equal after folding; or both close and the release year equal; or title and year equal (a classical recording credited to the composer on one side, the performers on the other). An equal id with other names is not a match: the feature table gave "Silent Hill" the id of "Silent Hill 2".
2. otherwise, folded artist, folded title and release year are all equal. Artists are compared under each spelling (the bracketed romanisation, the sheet's `artist_latin`, the corrected artist of `overrides.json`). When no row has the album's artist, a row with the same title and year whose credit shares a billed name is taken ("Bob Marley & The Wailers" and "The Wailers") and listed.

Never a title alone across artists, never a title that extends another (a sequel, an edition, a live version), never a year that differs unless the Spotify id and the names agree. An album pairs with one row and a row with one album; when two candidates compete, or the Spotify id says one row and artist, title and year another, nothing is paired. The release year of an existing album comes from the scrape row with its artist and title; when two albums share those (two self-titled albums), the table's order, which is the scrape's, says which row it is.

**Deciding a doubtful pair, or giving an album its RYM id.** Edit the album's row of `audio/keys.csv`: put the right `rym_id` (or its placeholder, to say it is not that chart row) and set `matched_by` to `manual`. Then run the builder, which keeps rows set by hand and rewrites the catalog and the doubtful list around them, and `scripts/rekey_audio_store.py`, which renames the album in the store. A placeholder that became a RYM id is found by itself; when one RYM id replaces another, give the script the previous file (`git show HEAD:data-pipeline/audio/keys.csv > /tmp/keys.csv`, then `--previous /tmp/keys.csv`). When the album already had a row of its own in the catalog (the chart row was a new album), the builder drops that row; delete its row from `matches.csv` before the rekey, which refuses to give two rows one key. The one-pass clip cache is not committed and is brought along separately: write the renames and the dropped listings into `.cache/audio/pending_cache_rekey.csv` (`action`, `old_key`, `new_key`, `source`, `album_id`, `note`) and run `scripts/apply_cache_rekey.py`, which only counts until it is given `--apply` and does not start while a one-pass run holds the cache's lock. The script rewrites keys only: `tests/test_rekey.py` checks every album's store row (bit for bit) and audio block (exactly) against `tests/fixtures/audio_reference.npz`, recorded before the first rekey by `scripts/record_audio_reference.py`. Record that file again only after a change meant to move the blocks (a refit, a top-up, a corrected match).

## Covers of the new albums

An existing album takes its cover id and its sprite from the map (`mapsource.py`, `images.py`). A new album has neither, so `rmr_pipeline/covers.py` finds one image per new album and records it in `catalog/covers.csv` (committed): `rym_id,source,ref`. It does the same for an existing album whose Spotify link on the sheet is another release than its `legacy_uri` (1,150 albums; see "The sheet's Spotify link" under Catalog mode): the cover follows the link. Such an album is only asked from Spotify, with the sheet's link; when Spotify has no answer it keeps the map's cover. Their rows come first in `covers.csv`, as the existing albums do in the catalog. The sources, best first:

| `source` | Where the image comes from | `ref` | `c` in `albums.json` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `spotify` | The catalog's Spotify link, asked from Spotify's oEmbed endpoint (no account) | The image id, as the site stores it today | `<id>` |
| `deezer` | The album's store listing: the one `match_overrides.json` forces, else the one in `audio/matches.csv`, else the catalog's Deezer link | Deezer's image md5 | `dz:<md5>` |
| `apple` | The same, for a listing on iTunes, else the catalog's Apple Music link | The path of Apple's artwork | `am:<path>` |
| `bandcamp` | The `og:image` of the catalog's Bandcamp page | The image's number | `bc:<number>` |
| `youtube` | The catalog's YouTube link, else the link the audio stage embedded (`audio/fulllength.csv`) | The video id. The image is a frame of the video, not a cover | `yt:<video id>` |
| `caa` | The last resort, in a table of its own (`catalog/covers_caa.csv`): the Cover Art Archive's front image of the album's release group on MusicBrainz | The release group's MBID | `ca:<mbid>` |

`covers.cover_url(c, px)` documents the URL of each form at a given size; the frontend mirrors it. An album with no row in either table has `c` `""`.

**The last resort: MusicBrainz and the Cover Art Archive** (tier `caa`; 6 October 2026, after the owner noticed the 163 albums without a cover). It is for every catalog album that would otherwise end with no cover, and never replaces a cover an album has:

- a new album with no row in `covers.csv` and no tier left that could give it one (60), one whose row's image is gone (22), one whose row is on the skip list (11);
- an existing album of `catalog/unverified_links.csv` (67: the cover its old link gave was another album's), and one `overrides.json` gives `c` `""` (3: Chill Out, Gimix, Dark & Long).

Its rows are in `catalog/covers_caa.csv` (committed): `rym_id,mbid,mb_title,mb_artist,mb_year,mb_type,score`, the release group's MBID and what was matched (the last five columns are for the reader; the build reads the first two). They are not rows of `covers.csv`, which does not take the source: the row an album has there stays (so `refs` does not find the same frame again and the skip list still names it), and `cover_for` answers from `covers_caa.csv` only when `covers.csv` gives the album nothing. When a better source later gives the album a usable row, that row wins by itself.

- *The lookup.* MusicBrainz's release-group search, `https://musicbrainz.org/ws/2/release-group/?query=...&fmt=json`, with the Lucene query `releasegroup:"<title>" AND artist:"<artist>"`: the catalog's native spelling (and the sheet's, where an existing album's differs), then the Latin forms (`title_latin`, `artist_latin`), then each with the first billed artist alone for a credit of several names, then the title alone (`releasegroup:"<title>"`: the search's artist field does not know every name of an artist, while an answer carries the artists' aliases). The searches stop at the first answer with an accepted group. Then `https://coverartarchive.org/release-group/<mbid>`, the archive's list of the group's images: HTTP 404, or a list with no front image, is "no art".
- *The rule* (`covers.mb_accepts`, `mb_choose`) is strict, because a wrong cover is worse than a tile. A release group is accepted only when its title equals the album's after folding (`covers.fold_title`: case, accents, punctuation, "&"/"and", a leading "the", bracketed edition suffixes such as "(Remastered 2011)"; the folding of `rmr_audio/textnorm.py`, which belongs to the audio environment) in one of the catalog's spellings, AND one of its credited artists (as credited, by its own name, or by an alias) equals one of the album's billed artists after folding, AND, when the catalog has a year and MusicBrainz a first-release date, the years are at most one apart. Of several accepted groups, those whose title is the album's as written are kept when there are any ("0%" and "0" fold alike), then those of the best type (Album, then EP, then Single, then the rest); when the ones left look alike (the same credit, year and secondary types) the earliest is taken, and when two look different (a studio and a live album, two years, two credits) none is: "ambiguous".
- *The record.* What was decided for every album asked is in `.cache/covers/state.json` under `caa`: `decision` (`found`, `none`, `ambiguous`, `no art`), `why`, MusicBrainz's `score`, the number of searches and the release group when there is one. A rerun asks for no album that has a decision there or a row in `covers_caa.csv`; `--retry-failed` asks again for those that were not found. A request that got no usable answer is not recorded, and the album is asked again by the next run.
- *MusicBrainz's rate rule.* At most one request a second (`CAA_INTERVALS`: 1.1 s between two requests to musicbrainz.org, 1 s to coverartarchive.org), with the User-Agent that names the project and its GitHub address. HTTP 503 is MusicBrainz's rate-limit answer: the request is tried once more after 30 seconds (`MB_BUSY_WAIT`), and a second 503 stops the run with exit code 2.
- *The `ca:` form.* `c` is `ca:<mbid>` (the MBID is 36 characters: lowercase hex and hyphens). `https://coverartarchive.org/release-group/<mbid>/front-250` is the image at up to 250 px and `/front-500` above. Both answer with a redirect to `archive.org`, which redirects to a host under `archive.org` (`dn710603.ca.archive.org`, `ia800509.us.archive.org`), so the allow-list has exactly `coverartarchive.org`, `archive.org`, `*.archive.org` and `musicbrainz.org` for it, and a redirect anywhere else is refused as before. The sprite is made from `front-250`. The archive's storage hosts answer HTTP 500 for some files at times (6 of the release groups on 6 October 2026, each of them fine at a later try the same evening): such a request is not recorded and is asked again.
- *Sprites.* `sprites` fetches the image of every row of `covers_caa.csv` that stands in for no row, a skipped row or an image that is gone, and records it in the manifest as `caa:<mbid>`. The sprite of an existing album with no RYM id (key `sp:<Spotify id>`) is the file `sp_<Spotify id>.jpg`. A failed image is recorded under `caa_sprites` in the state file, apart from the row's own failure, and then gives no cover.
- *In the build.* A new album gets the cover through `cover_for` like any other. An existing album whose `c` is empty once `unverified_links.csv` and `overrides.json` have been applied takes it through `catalog.last_resort_covers`: an album of `unverified_links.csv` takes the cover id with its sprite and the ambient colours of that sprite (its `s` stays empty), and waits on the tile without a sprite, since a cover id with no file of its own would put the map's sprite, the wrong album's, on the sheets; an album with an image of its own in `overrides.json` (the 3) takes the cover id alone, so the album page can show the archive's image, while its sprite and its ambient colours stay the override image's. `--require-sprites` stops the build when a row has no sprite.
- *Taking one out, or correcting one.* A wrong cover: add `rym_id,caa,<mbid>,<what the image is>` to `covers_skip.csv`; the row stays in `covers_caa.csv` and gives no cover, and the lookup does not find it again. Another release group: put its MBID in the album's row and run `sprites`. A release group the lookup did not accept: add the row by hand. `scripts/caa_review.py` writes `docs/review/caa-covers.jpg` and `.md` (every such cover, numbered, with what it was matched to) and `docs/review/still-no-cover.md`.

On 6 October 2026 it asked for the 163 albums (about 450 requests for the lookups and 135 for the sprites, over several runs; no 503 and no refusal from MusicBrainz): 123 found (58 of the 67 unverified links, the 3 of `overrides.json`, 40 of the 60 with no source, 14 of the 22 gone, 8 of the 11 skipped), 39 none, 0 ambiguous, 1 no art.

**The skip list.** The YouTube frames are approved as covers (the owner, 6 October 2026, on `docs/review/youtube-covers.jpg`), except the frames that are plainly not one: a "FULL ALBUM" card, sheet music, a track list, a near-black or near-blank frame. Those are named in `catalog/covers_skip.csv` (committed, written by hand): `rym_id,source,ref,note`, 11 rows today. A row of `covers.csv` with that album, source and ref gives no cover: `cover_for` returns no `c` and no sprite, and the album shows the lettered tile. The row stays in `covers.csv`, so `refs` does not find the same image again; `sprites` does not fetch it; `status` and the build count the skipped rows. When the album later gets another source or ref, the skip no longer applies. To skip another image, add its line with a note saying what the image is.

```bash
cd data-pipeline
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.covers refs --dry-run   # what a run would ask, per tier; no request, nothing written
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.covers refs             # (re)writes catalog/covers.csv, then catalog/covers_caa.csv (the last resort)
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.covers refs --tiers caa # the last resort alone: MusicBrainz at one request a second
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.covers sprites          # one 96 px JPEG per row in .cache/covers/96/
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.covers status           # rows per source, sprites present and missing, albums without a cover
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.covers adopt --dry-run  # the sprites with no entry in the manifest; without --dry-run, records them
```

`refs` works in tiers, in this order: `spotify`, `cache`, `store`, `bandcamp`, `youtube`, then `caa`, the last resort above, which runs only when the others ended without a stop (`--tiers` to choose). `cache` reads the Deezer and iTunes listings from the audio stage's response cache (`.cache/audio/http.sqlite`, read only) and uses no network; `store` asks the store for a listing the cache lacks; `youtube` uses no network either. A tier never spends a request on an album that a better source may still answer: Bandcamp is not asked while the album's Spotify lookup is neither done nor failed. The `cache` tier is the exception, since it costs nothing: it gives every album it can a row at once, and the Spotify tier replaces that row later.

Both commands can be stopped and started again. `refs` saves `covers.csv` every 50 albums and skips the albums that have their row; `sprites` skips the albums that have a sprite of their row's image. An album whose lookup failed for a reason of its own (HTTP 404 or 410, a page with no image, a redirect to a host that is not allowed) is written to `.cache/covers/state.json` and not asked again without `--retry-failed`; a refused or unanswered request, or any other status, is not written there, so the next run asks again. A row of `covers.csv` whose album the `--albums` file does not look up (not a new album, nor an existing one with another Spotify link on the sheet) is kept, after the others, and `refs` prints how many there are.

One `refs` run and one `sprites` run at a time: each holds a lock file for as long as it runs (`.cache/covers/refs.lock`, `sprites.lock`), and a second run of the same command prints who holds it and exits with code 1. `status`, `--dry-run` and the build take no lock.

Requests go only to the hosts in `ALLOWED_HOSTS` and `ALLOWED_SUFFIXES` of `covers.py` (the five services' pages, APIs and image hosts, and MusicBrainz, the Cover Art Archive and archive.org for the last resort; never rateyourmusic.com), a redirect to another host is refused, requests are spaced per host, and the User-Agent names the project. Only https on the default port is asked. HTTP 403 or 429 twice in a row from one host, or five times in a run, stops the run with exit code 2; Deezer's quota error, which comes as a 200, counts as one; so does a second HTTP 503 from MusicBrainz.

The sprites live in `.cache/covers/96/<rym_id>.jpg` and are not committed: `covers.csv` is the record, and `sprites` makes them again from it. `sprites` also writes `.cache/covers/96.manifest.json`: for each sprite, the `source:ref` it was made from. A sprite whose entry is not its row's image (the row changed and the old file survived) counts as missing: the build does not use it and `sprites` makes it again. A sprite with no entry (made before the manifest existed, or by a run that was killed between the file and the entry) is used as it is; `status` and the build print how many there are, and `adopt` records them as made from their rows. `adopt` makes no request, never changes an entry, and leaves alone a sprite whose file is older than `covers.csv`, since the table may have changed after it was made (`--older-too` adopts those as well).

The build reads all of this through `covers.cover_for(key)`. An album with a row and no sprite of that image keeps its `c` and gets the tile of its cluster on the sheets, with the fallback ambient colours. A tile on the sheets is lettered like the album page's tile (`images.lettered_tile`: the cluster's fill, a 1 px lighter inner border, and the letter `images.tile_letter` takes from the shown title, in the committed `assets/DejaVuSerif.ttf`, licence beside it), so an album without a cover does not look like a failed image on the map (40 albums today, those the last resort found nothing for: 31 new ones, 1 of them with a title that is not Latin and no Latin form, which gets the bordered tile with no letter, and 9 of the 67 existing albums of `catalog/unverified_links.csv`; the 3 existing albums whose `c` `overrides.json` empties have an image of their own on the sheets, as before). The letter rule is the frontend's (`initialLetter`): change both together. A `--no-catalog` build's tiles stay flat. The exception is an album whose image `sprites` could not fetch for a reason of the image's own (the state file's `sprites` entry names exactly its row's `source:ref`: HTTP 404 or 410, a file that is not an image): it has no cover from that row, since the site would ask for that image first and fail: it shows the last resort's cover when `covers_caa.csv` has one for it, else its `c` is empty and it shows the lettered tile like an album with no row. The build prints how many there are, and they do not stop `--require-sprites`. `covers.csv` keeps the row; when `refs` gives the album another image, it is asked again. The build prints a `WARNING, covers:` line when `covers.csv` is missing (no new album has a cover then), when albums have a cover and no sprite, and when sprites have no manifest entry; `--require-sprites` makes each of the three an error.

## Catalog mode

Catalog mode is the build's default since 6 October 2026, and what the site's data is built with: `python -m rmr_pipeline.build --map-root <map>` builds every album of `catalog/albums.csv` (10,467), keyed by RYM id (`rmr_pipeline/catalog.py`), from the `effnet10k` store unless `--audio-dir` says otherwise, into `frontcreck/public/data` unless `--out` says otherwise. `--catalog` is still accepted and changes nothing. `--no-catalog` builds the feature table's 4,081 albums alone, as before the switch; it needs an explicit `--out` and refuses `frontcreck/public/data`, and the flags of this section are not for it. The existing albums keep their rows and slugs; the new albums follow, and a new album's slug is made from its romanised title and artist when the catalog has them. A new album's slug is at most 120 bytes (`slugs.MAX_SLUG_BYTES`), collision suffix included: a longer one is cut at word boundaries, the title's part to 80 characters or what a short artist leaves, the artist's part to the rest (17 albums today). A slug is a file name on the host, which fails the deploy over 233 bytes; `validate.py` refuses any slug over 200.

```bash
cd data-pipeline
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.covers adopt --dry-run                                  # then without --dry-run: see below
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.build --require-sprites --map-root /path/to/music_map   # frontcreck/public/data; about 100 seconds; slope weights, vocals dropped
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.validate
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.build --map-root /path/to/music_map --out .cache/try    # a trial build elsewhere
.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.validate --data .cache/try
```

What the data holds, where it differs from a `--no-catalog` build's (the site's data until the switch):

- **`albums.json`**: `slug, t, a, s, c, k, d, w`, then `l` when the album has one, then `n` when it has one.
  - `c` is `""`, a Spotify image id, or one of the prefixed forms above (`dz:`, `am:`, `bc:`, `yt:`, `ca:`; see Covers of the new albums). An existing album has a prefixed one only as a last resort (`ca:`).
  - `a`: an existing album's artist is the feature table's, cleaned. A new album's is the catalog's `artist`, or `native [Latin]` (`파란노을 [Parannoul]`, the form 33 existing albums have) when the credit has a letter that is not in Latin script and `artist_latin` is there and different (`catalog.display_artist`; 508 new albums). A credit of several names gets one bracket with the romanisation as the catalog gives it, which can leave out a collaborator or the names that were Latin already (`菅野よう子 & Seatbelts [Yoko Kanno]`; 100 such credits). A credit that carries its brackets already, or has no `artist_latin`, is left as it is. Slugs are made from the romanised forms alone, as before, and the site's search finds either spelling.
  - `t`: an existing album's title is the feature table's. A new album's is the catalog's `title`, or `title [Latin]` (`ヴィジョン クリエイション ニューサン [Vision Creation Newsun]`) when the title has a letter that is not in Latin script and `title_latin` is there and different (`catalog.display_title`; the owner's decision of 6 October 2026; 456 new albums). `title_latin` is used as given: a romanisation for some albums, an English translation for others. A title that carries a Latin form of its own in round or square brackets is left as it is (`Mother (マザー)`, `勝訴ストリップ (Shōso Strip)`); a bracket with no letters, or one the Latin form repeats (`静香 (III)`), does not count as one.
  - `s`, the sheet's Spotify link: an existing album's Spotify id is the one of its `spotify_url` in the catalog when it has one (3,557 of 4,081), else the one of its `legacy_uri`, the feature table's (the owner's decision of 6 October 2026: "the sheet is more up to date than the existing albums"). An entry of `overrides.json` wins over both (its `s`, `c` and `image`, as before). For 1,150 albums the sheet's id is another than the table's, 13 of them decided by an override. An audit against Spotify found that where the two differ the table's id is another edition of the album or, often, another album (21 of the 26 ids an existing and a new album shared; 3 of a random 40 of the others), and that the sheet's was right in every case checked. The other 1,137 take the cover of the release they now link to: `c` from their row of `covers.csv`, the sprite from `.cache/covers/96`, the ambient colours from that sprite. One with no row or no sprite (Spotify had no answer, the image is gone, the row is skipped) keeps the map's cover id and sprite; the build prints how many (none today). An album whose id does not change keeps the map's cover. The `URI` the map and the feature table are read by does not change.
  - `s`, the unverified links: `catalog/unverified_links.csv` (committed, written by hand: `rym_id,slug,site_id,opens,note`) lists 67 existing albums whose Spotify link opens another album and which have no link on the sheet, so the right id is not known (section A of `docs/review/spotify-links-needs-owner.md`); the build gives them `s` `""` and no Spotify cover, and they keep the catalog's other links as `l` (23 of them have one). 58 of them show the Cover Art Archive's cover instead (`c` `ca:<mbid>`, its sprite and its colours; see The last resort); the other 9 have `c` `""`, the lettered tile on the sheets and the fallback ambient colours. A `spotify_url` on the sheet or an `s` in `overrides.json` for the album wins over its row, so adding the right link there fixes the album without touching the list; the build prints how many rows it applied and names each row that no longer applies, and deleting a row (or the file) gives the album back the feature table's link and the map's cover.
  - `d` has up to 8 indexes, in the order of the album's RYM page. Every album is described by its first 8 descriptors. `--existing-descriptors` says where an existing album's come from: `table-novocals` (default: the 8 largest table cells with the three vocals descriptors left out), `table` (vocals kept) or `sheet` (the catalog's `top_descriptors`, as for a new album). The vocals descriptors are dropped for every album by the owner's decision of 6 October 2026: the sheet does not list them, so only the existing albums would carry them (2,772 have one among their 8, no new album does), and with them kept new albums took 32% of the mood top-10 places of existing albums against 49% without. To be revisited if the sheet gains vocals for all albums. `--descriptor-weights` weights them by place among the 8: `slope` (default: 1 down to 0.5; the owner's decision of 6 October 2026, after comparing it with `rank`, see `experiments/top8_descriptors/REPORT.md` section 6), `rank` (the table's `1.5 - place / 42`) or `equal`; `slope` and `equal` are scaled to the length of a full `rank` row. A sheet name that is not a table column is looked up in `catalog/descriptor_aliases.json` (RYM renames) and dropped otherwise; the build prints the counts.
  - `k`: a new album takes the cluster of most of its 5 nearest existing albums, at the balanced stop, or by descriptors alone when it has no audio. An existing album with no audio does not vote at the balanced stop: its block there is the mean block, not its own.
  - `l`, the listen links beyond Spotify, is there only when `s` is empty and the catalog has another link for the album. It is an object with any of these keys, in this order, each a short ref made from the catalog's link column (`rmr_pipeline/links.py`; `link_url(service, ref)` gives the page back):

    | Key | Catalog column | Ref | URL |
    |---|---|---|---|
    | `am` | `apple_music_url` | `<storefront>/<numeric album id>` | `https://music.apple.com/<storefront>/album/<id>` |
    | `bc` | `bandcamp_url` | host and path, for example `magdalenabay.bandcamp.com/album/imaginal-disk` | `https://<ref>` |
    | `dz` | `deezer_url` | the numeric album id | `https://www.deezer.com/album/<id>` |
    | `yt` | `youtube_url` | the video id | `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>` |
    | `sc` | `soundcloud_url` | the path, for example `radiohead/sets/ok-computer-3` | `https://soundcloud.com/<ref>` |

    Query strings are dropped. A Bandcamp page can be on a label's own domain, so `bc` keeps the host; since the site opens `https://<ref>`, the host must be `<name>.bandcamp.com` or one of `links.BANDCAMP_HOSTS`, the 20 custom domains the catalog has today (a test compares the list with `catalog/albums.csv`, so a new one has to be added by hand). A link that is not of its service's form, or a Bandcamp link on another host, is left out, and the build prints how many were. An existing album whose `s` is empty (an override took it away, or the album is in `unverified_links.csv`) gets `l` by the same rule. Today 844 albums have no Spotify id (774 new, 70 existing): 719 have `l` (YouTube 609, Bandcamp 285, Apple Music 122, SoundCloud 108, Deezer 30) and 125 have no link at all (79 new, 46 existing).
  - `n` is `1` when the album has no audio in the store the build reads, and absent otherwise.
- **Albums without audio are limited to the mood side** (the owner's decision of 6 October 2026; it replaces imputation, which a `--no-catalog` build keeps). Nothing is imputed. In `recs.json` an `n` album's `sonic` and `balanced` rows are `[]` and no other album's `sonic` or `balanced` row lists it: those two stops rank the albums with audio among themselves. The `mood` stop ranks every album among all of them, so an `n` album has a normal list of 10 there and appears in other albums' lists. Its audio block at that stop is the mean block of the albums with audio, a neutral value where the block has almost no weight (`audio.mean_fill`). Two `n` albums would then be at audio distance 0 from each other and recommend each other too often, so the mood lists (not the mood map) are ranked on a matrix with one more column per `n` album, holding sqrt(V) on that album's row, V being the mean squared distance of a block from the mean block over the albums with audio (`recs.no_audio_columns`; not divided by the slider term, like the block). A pair of an `n` album and an album with audio pays V more, a pair of two `n` albums 2V, the average for two albums with audio. On the catalog of 6 October the share of the `n` albums' mood places that hold another `n` album goes from 4.5% to 3.6% (3.5% by descriptors alone; `n` albums are 2.1% of the catalog), and 266 of 10,467 mood lists change.
- **`positions.json`** has the shape it always had: every album has a position at all three stops. The sonic and balanced UMAPs are fitted on the albums with audio only. An `n` album is then put, on each of those two maps, at the mean position of its 3 nearest albums with audio by descriptor distance (`layout.nearest_with_audio`), once the islands, the outliers and the box of that map are settled and before the alignment, the resizing and the spreading of stacked points, so positions stay unique. The mood UMAP is fitted on every album.
- **Sprites**: `atlas-N.webp` (96 px, 32 columns, 1,024 per sheet), 11 sheets for 10,467 albums. Thumbnails are 48 px, 64 x 64 = 4,096 per sheet: sheet 0 is `thumbs.webp`, then `thumbs-1.webp`, `thumbs-2.webp`; album i is in sheet floor(i / 4096) at cell i % 4096. Numbered sheets left in the folder by a larger build are removed. Every number is a constant in `constants.py`, and the frontend has the same ones.

The build prints the albums with and without audio (existing and new), the existing albums that take their id from the sheet and those whose cover follows it or stays the map's, the new albums with a cover, with a sprite and with a cover but no sprite yet, the skipped rows, the new albums shown with a bracketed artist or title, the rows of `unverified_links.csv` it applied, and the counts of the links. It also prints the Spotify ids that more than one album has, counted on the ids the build ends with (12 today, 5 of them between an existing and a new album, 1 between two existing ones; 32 with the feature table's ids), a line per id with each album's number, RYM id, artist, title and side: both albums would open the same Spotify album, so one link is wrong or the two rows are one release. The build does not fail on them or change them; `docs/review/shared-spotify-ids.md` lists them for the owner. It compares its slugs with the committed `frontcreck/public/data/albums.json` (read before anything is written) and stops if one would change or an album of that file is gone; since the switch the file has the new albums too, so a new album's slug is as fixed as an existing one's. `--require-sprites` stops it when `covers.csv` is missing, when a new album, or an existing one whose link changed, has a cover in it and no sprite of that image, or when a sprite has no manifest entry (for the final build). Before it, once no `sprites` run is going: `python -m rmr_pipeline.covers adopt --dry-run`, then `adopt`, which records the sprites a run without the manifest left; delete the sprites it leaves alone as older than `covers.csv` and run `sprites` again. `--skip-images` needs an explicit `--out` (see Commands). `validate.py` checks all of the above: the shape of `l` and `n`, the cover id forms, `[]` at sonic and balanced exactly for the `n` albums, no `n` album in a sonic or balanced row, and one thumbnail sheet per 4,096 albums.

`pytest` here runs `tests/` (the build). The tests of the audio stage and of the catalog builder are in `tests_audio/` and run with the audio venv (see The audio stage); `tests/test_audio_store.py`, `tests/test_keys.py` and `tests/test_rekey.py` pass in both.

The pinned requirements need Python 3.12.

`--hub-correction balanced` (any comma-separated stops) ranks those stops by mutual proximity instead of the raw distance: an album that is close to everything stops counting as close. At the balanced stop the most-recommended album goes from 92 lists to 37, the albums in no list from 4.8% to 0.9%, and about a third of the recommendations change. It is off by default because the lists then differ from "nearest on the map".

`--skip-images` is a faster development run that keeps the fallback ambient colours and writes no sprite sheets. It needs an explicit `--out` folder so it never overwrites the committed `albums.json`; check that folder with `.venv/bin/python -m rmr_pipeline.validate --data <folder> --no-images`.

`--no-catalog --out <folder>` builds the feature table's 4,081 albums alone, with an imputed block for an album without audio, up to 10 descriptors per album and flat tiles: the build of before the switch. It reads the store of `SITE_MODEL` like any build, so the data the site had needs `--audio-dir audio`. It never writes into `frontcreck/public/data`. See Catalog mode for what the default build adds.

The sheets a build no longer needs are removed from the output folder: a numbered `atlas-N.webp` or `thumbs-N.webp` past the last one written. Every other file is overwritten in place.

UMAP output depends on the exact versions of umap-learn, pynndescent and numba; keep `requirements.txt` pinned so the layouts stay reproducible.

## Overrides

`overrides.json` holds the corrections of a verified list kept in the owner's personal-site repository (`<personal-site-repo>/scripts/music-catalog/cover-fixes.json`): albums whose Spotify URI in the feature table points at a different album (for example Joni Mitchell, "Blue" pointed at a tribute single). Each key is the slug the pipeline derives from the feature table (after artist cleaning, before any correction). Fields, all strings, all optional:

- `s`: the right Spotify album id (22 characters), or `""` when the album has no Spotify release.
- `c`: the right cover id (the last part of its `https://i.scdn.co/image/` URL), or `""` when there is no Spotify cover. A cover correction needs `image`.
- `a`: the credited artist, when the feature table has the wrong one. The album's slug is rebuilt from it; the key stays the feature-table slug. The build fails if the new slug would change any other album's slug.
- `image`: a replacement cover image relative to this folder (`overrides/<slug>.jpg`), used for the sprites and the ambient colours. The images are cropped from the personal site's cover atlas, or taken from its Cover Art Archive covers for the albums with no Spotify release. An album whose `c` is `""` here gets `ca:<mbid>` from `catalog/covers_caa.csv` when the last resort found its release group (all 3 today): the album page then has a remote image to show, and the sprite stays this `image`.
- `note`: why (the wrong album the URI points at, and the right one).

`overrides.py` checks every field and names the entry and the field in its error. Example:

```json
{ "blue-joni-mitchell": { "s": "<22-character album id>", "c": "<cover id>", "image": "overrides/blue-joni-mitchell.jpg", "note": "Spotify URI points at a tribute single" } }
```

An existing album's Spotify id comes from the sheet when the sheet has a link (see Catalog mode); an entry here still wins over the sheet and over the feature table, field by field, and an album with an `s`, `c` or `image` here keeps the cover this file or the map gives it.

Only the Spotify link, the cover, the sprites, the ambient colours and (for `a`) the artist and slug change. All 19 corrections of the list match an album in this catalog. Their positions and recommendations no longer use the wrong album's audio: preview clips are matched by artist and title, not by Spotify URI. All 19 have audio in `audio/effnet10k` (in `audio/`, 15 of them; the other 4 were imputed).

## Known data problems (documented, not fixed)

- 34 Spotify URIs in the feature table are assigned to two or three different albums (69 rows). The pipeline keeps the first row and drops the other 35.
- Some Spotify URIs in the feature table point at a different album. The verified ones are corrected in `overrides.json` (see Overrides). The album's key in the audio store is its RYM id, not the URI (see Catalog and keys).
- 225 albums have no audio and are limited to the mood side: 39 existing, 186 new (see Albums without audio).
- Audio matches are not all right: in the experiment's hand audit of the first store about 1% of confident matches and about a third of the 148 then flagged `ambiguous` in `matches.csv` were the wrong album or edition. A new album's listing reached through the catalog's Deezer or Apple link is accepted without a check (see Matching).
- An album's vector is the mean of four clips (99 albums on previews have fewer, 27 of them one). The first store had up to eight.
- 67 existing albums have no Spotify link, because the link they had opens another album and the right one is not known (`catalog/unverified_links.csv`; "One" by Neal Morse, which showed a Neal Francis sleeve, is one of them). 44 of them have no listen link of any kind. 58 show the Cover Art Archive's cover; 9 have no cover. Section B of `docs/review/spotify-links-needs-owner.md` (4 albums whose sheet link is a larger release that holds the album) and section C (a sampler) keep their links.
- 12 Spotify ids are shared by two albums each, so one of each pair opens the wrong album or the two rows are one release (`docs/review/shared-spotify-ids.md`).
- The 1,137 existing albums whose link the sheet changed show the cover of the release they now link to, which for most is another edition's sleeve (`docs/review/changed-covers.jpg`).
- "Chill Out" (The KLF), "Gimix" (The Avalanches) and "Dark & Long" (Underworld) have no Spotify release, so they have no Spotify id and no cover id. Chill Out has YouTube and SoundCloud links; the other two have no link. Their sprites and ambient colours use the corrected cover of `overrides.json`. Since 6 October 2026 their `c` is the Cover Art Archive's (`ca:<mbid>`, the release groups the override images were taken from), so the album page can show the cover once the frontend knows the `ca:` form (its cover component shows a cover id's image, and the sprite only when that image fails).
- 40 albums have no cover and show the lettered tile (`docs/review/still-no-cover.md` lists them with the links the catalog has): 31 new ones (20 with no source, 8 whose image could not be fetched and 3 whose YouTube frame is not a cover; 60, 22 and 11 before the last resort) and 9 existing ones of `unverified_links.csv`. For 39 of the 40 MusicBrainz has no release group that passes the rule, and for 1 the archive has no image.
- 123 albums show a Cover Art Archive cover found by title, artist and year (`docs/review/caa-covers.jpg`); the rule is strict but nobody has checked each one against the record.
- 341 new albums show a frame of a YouTube video as their cover (approved by the owner).
- 96 albums have no mood descriptors (73 existing, 23 new). An album shows at most 8 words, 5.9 on average; an existing album showed up to 10 before the switch (7.7 on average, 5.6 now).
- 957 descriptor names on the sheet have no column in the feature table and are dropped (187 different names; "harsh vocals", "jamming" and "unaccompanied solo" are the most frequent), and 51 albums have no descriptor at all in the matrix.
- The feature table has 176 descriptor columns (the design spec says 175).
- Cluster ids come from the map pipeline and are unbalanced (three clusters hold almost every album); the site only uses them for dot colours.
- Artist cleaning only drops a glued tail that repeats a name from the credit. A credit glued to an unrelated name, or to member names spelled too differently, would stay as it is; none is known in the current table. The member names in the dropped tail are not kept.
- The new albums have no cluster of their own: each takes the cluster of its nearest existing albums (the site only uses clusters for dot colours and tile colours).
- Adding an album to `audio/effnet10k` needs a refit of the transform, which moves every block (see The block).
